# 100-car EV fleet: Tesla Model Y vs Ford Mustang Mach-E

Executive summary
- Key tradeoffs: range, cargo space, total cost of ownership, and charging/network availability. Based on recent reviews and manufacturer data, the Model Y Long Range AWD offers the best range and cargo capacity in this comparison, while the Mach-E provides strong value with competitive range at a lower upfront price on some trims and a broad dealer/service footprint.


## Quick specs snapshot (highlights)
- Tesla Model Y Long Range AWD (Juniper update, 2026 data)
  - EPA range: up to 327 miles with 19" wheels; about 303 miles with 20" wheels
  - Cargo space: behind rear seat ~29 cu ft; behind front seat ~71 cu ft
  - Typical price range: base models around $41k; longer-range variants commonly in the $50k–$61k territory depending on wheels and options
  - Charging: Tesla Supercharger network; strong highway charging performance

- Ford Mustang Mach-E (2024–2026 data, Extended Range variants)
  - EPA range: generally 240–320 miles depending on trim and battery; Extended Range RWD/Premium AWD can reach up to ~320 miles
  - Cargo space: behind rear seat ~29.7 cu ft; behind first row ~59.7 cu ft
  - Typical price range: from about $39.8k to $60k+ depending on trim (GT higher)
  - Charging: DC fast charging up to 150 kW; Ford BlueOval Charge Network integration


## Fleet-focused comparison
- Range and daily duty cycle
  - Model Y Long Range AWD generally offers the best real-world range in this pair, aiding long highway routes and fewer charging stops per day.
  - Mach-E Extended Range variants close in on range, with some trims offering up to 320 miles per charge; the actual range varies by wheel size and driving conditions.

- Cargo and interior flexibility
  - Model Y: larger combined cargo envelope in many configurations, with 71 cu ft of cargo space behind the front seats and 29 cu ft behind the rear seats, enabling more bulky equipment or gear.
  - Mach-E: slightly smaller cargo space behind rear seats (≈29.7 cu ft) but still adequate for many fleet tasks; total interior volume is competitive.

- Upfront cost and depreciation tendencies
  - Base price for Model Y varies by trim; typical starting point around mid $40k, with higher trims approaching $60k+.
  - Mach-E starts lower on entry trims (roughly $39k–$45k) and can exceed $60k for GT/Rally trims.
  - Residual values and depreciation depend on market, incentives, and fleet utilization; both brands have active used/EOL markets.

- Charging and service ecosystem
  - Tesla: extensive Supercharger network, fast charging performance, strong uptime in many regions. Requires considerations for vehicle service center density.
  - Ford: broad dealer/service network; Ford’s BlueOval charging network offers integrated home and public charging; 150 kW peak DC fast charging on many extended-range trims.

- Warranty and maintenance outlook
  - Both offer competitive EV warranties (battery and powertrain). Specific terms should be confirmed for fleet contracts and extended warranty options.


## Recommendation for a 100-vehicle fleet
- Primary recommendation: Model Y Long Range AWD
  - Why: marginally higher EPA range (327 miles vs up to about 320 miles in top Mach-E extended-range trims), larger usable cargo space, and robust highway charging performance. This combination lowers downtime due to charging and increases daily route flexibility for highway-heavy duty.
  - Caveats: Tesla network and service availability in your operating regions; total cost of ownership may be higher for some trims, depending on options and incentives.

- Secondary consideration: Mach-E for lower upfront cost and strong regional dealer/service coverage
  - When to consider: if upfront capex is a dominant constraint or if your operating regions favor Ford's service footprint and you operate more local routes with shorter daily ranges.
  - Recommendation: consider a blended approach (e.g., a 60/40 split favoring Model Y) to balance range/cargo advantages with cost and dealer coverage. Pilot performance with a subset of vehicles can help validate real-world TOM (Total Ownership Model) data for your fleet.
